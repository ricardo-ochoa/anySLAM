There are two levels of goals, and it is worth not conflating them: those of the **research
project** and those of the **documentation portal**.

## Research project goals

These goals are inferred from published work and from repository contents. They should be
confirmed with the people leading each line of work.

1. **Robust learned locomotion.** Train control policies in simulation (Isaac Lab) capable of
   traversing irregular terrain, and transfer them to the physical ANYmal D with the smallest
   possible sim-to-real gap.
2. **Multimodal perception.** Combine 3D LiDAR and RGB cameras for localisation and mapping.
3. **Generalisable navigation.** Generate collision-free trajectories in novel environments
   without retraining per scenario. This is the stated goal of the work presented at
   [ICRA 2025](/en/docs/09-research/papers).
4. **Real-time inference.** Run the models onboard, within the robot's compute budget.

## Portal goals

These are concrete and verifiable:

1. **Give general context** about the project to someone arriving cold.
2. **Document the full architecture** of the system.
3. **Maintain a catalog** of the repositories that compose it.
4. **Identify maintainers** for each component.
5. **Explain the interfaces** between hardware, software, sensors and services.
6. **Establish shared documentation standards** across teams.
7. **Ease onboarding** of new members.
8. **Prepare the ground** for automation and, later, a queryable knowledge index.

## How we measure whether the portal works

A single, checkable criterion:

> A new person, with no prior context, must be able to go from the home page to running the
> system without asking anyone anything.

If at any point they have to ask, that point is a documentation gap and deserves an issue. The
ten questions they must be able to answer are in
[the project map](/en/docs/08-onboarding/project-map).
